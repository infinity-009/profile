Tier 12 · Agentic systems and graph retrieval Text to query

Better models take text-to-query from 63% to 93% accurate, and take the share of its errors that answer a different question from 32% to 92%

Execution accuracy is one scalar over three failure classes with nothing in common: a query that will not run costs a retry, and a query that runs and answers a slightly different question costs a quarter of decisions. A better model fixes the first class almost completely and the third one barely, so the errors that survive are exactly the ones nothing downstream can see.

01

The dashboard that was right for a quarter

Someone asks the analytics assistant how many active users did we have last week. It writes SQL, the SQL runs, a number comes back, and the number goes on a slide. It is 8% high. It is 8% high every week for a quarter, so it moves the way the real number moves, and the trend everyone is reading off the chart is correct while the level is not.

The query filtered users.is_active = 1. That column means “the account has not been closed”. The business meaning of active is “logged in within twenty-eight days”, and it lives in a dashboard definition, a wiki page, and the head of a growth analyst — not in the schema. Nothing about the query is malformed. It parses, it binds, it uses the right join, and it answers a question that nobody asked.

The postmortem is where it gets uncomfortable, because every control the team had was aimed somewhere else. Execution accuracy did not catch it: the query executed. There was no unit test to fail, because a unit test needs a gold query and the whole point of the feature is that nobody wrote one. The generated SQL was displayed under the chart, in a collapsible panel, and the two people who opened it in three months could not read SQL. The model was upgraded twice during the quarter and the number stayed 8% high both times, because a better model reads the same schema.

02

The belief this breaks: 91% accurate means 9% errors

The published metric for this technique is execution accuracy: run the generated query, run a hand-written gold query, compare the rows. It is a good metric for benchmarking and it is a single scalar laid over three populations that have nothing operationally in common.

what happenedwhat it costswho finds out
the query will not runa retry, and a few hundred millisecondsthe system, immediately
it runs and returns nothinga confused user, and a retrythe user, immediately
it runs and returns plausible rowsa quarter of decisionsnobody

Read the table and “9% of the time we get an error” stops meaning anything. Nine percent of the time you get a result, and the question that matters is what share of those results announce themselves.

Here is what makes this more than a taxonomy. The three classes do not shrink together. Dialect and binding errors are pattern-matching over SQL text — the thing these models are best at, and the thing each generation improves most. A misread column needs information the schema does not contain, so a better model brings no new information to it. Two panels, one axis.

Predict A text-to-query system on a real, undocumented schema gets 63% of questions right today. Four generations of model improvement take it to 93%. What happens to the share of its remaining errors that run, return plausible rows, and answer a different question?
Graded in text-to-query.test.mjs, which asserts the rise is monotone across every schema rung. The classes have different elasticities: a dialect slip is in the model's training distribution and one generation quarters it, while the meaning of a status code is in a Slack thread from 2019 and a generation removes about 15% of those. Four generations cut the total error rate 5.4× and the silent error rate 1.9×, so silent errors go from 32.4% of the residue to 91.7% of it. Option 1 is the intuition everyone has. Option 2 would be true if all three classes had the same elasticity. Option 4 underestimates it because the exponentials separate faster than they feel like they should.
Show it without answering

The published numbers say the same thing from the other side. The same systems score around 86% on the curated schemas of a benchmark like Spider and around 63% on the real databases of one like BIRD — dirty values, cryptic column names, business logic parked in an integer. Human annotators working on those same real databases score around 92.5%. Twenty-three points of what gets reported as model accuracy is a property of the schema, and the human baseline says it is not an intrinsic limit of the task.

03

The intuition: the back-translator must not have seen the source

Take a translator into a negotiation. The translator who stops and says I do not know that word is safe — you have been told there is a gap, and you can work around it. The translator who does not know the word and produces a fluent guess is the dangerous one, because fluency is the only signal you have and it is now uninformative.

Professional translation has known this for a long time and has a protocol for it: back-translation. Someone renders the translated text back into the original language, and the two originals are compared. What matters is the fine print. The guidance for translating survey instruments and consent forms — the ISPOR task force’s process for patient-reported outcome measures, the WHO’s for its own instruments — specifies that the back-translator is a different person who has not seen the source text.

That clause is not bureaucratic caution. It is the entire value of the check. A translator back-translating their own work reproduces their own misreading, so the check comes back clean and the error is now endorsed. This is the shape to hold onto for the rest of the lesson: a check on a translation is worth exactly as much as the checker’s independence from the translator, and asking the same model on the same schema to confirm its own SQL is the self-back-translation the protocol exists to forbid.

04

The mechanism: two exponentials and a coin flip at one half

Why the mix inverts

Give each class its own response to a generation of model improvement, because that is what the evidence shows. Dialect and binding errors get roughly quartered — they are text patterns. Empty-result errors get halved: matching cancelled in the question to CANCELLED in the column is partly a text problem and partly a data problem. A misread column loses about 15%, because the missing information is not in the prompt and a better reader of an absent fact is still not reading it.

Three sums are worth reading off it. The total error rate falls 5.42×. The silent error rate falls 1.92×. And the curated-schema score of 86% sits at about +1.6 generations while the human score of 92.5% sits at about +3.6 — so a model four generations out is roughly at the level of a person reading the same undocumented columns today, and 92% of the errors it has left are ones the person would have asked a question about.

Why the vote cannot help

The standard reflex is self-consistency: generate k queries independently, execute them all, take the majority result set. It is order 75’s technique, applied to a new population, and it is worth working out what it does here rather than assuming.

An error is caught when fewer than a majority of the samples share it. So the only quantity that matters is the error’s recurrence — the chance an independent sample makes the same mistake. And the break-even is a clean identity: a majority vote catches exactly half of the errors that recur exactly half the time, at every odd sample count, because the binomial median sits at the mean when p = ½.

Below the line, more samples help. Above it, more samples make the majority surer of the same mistake. That single row flips the technique from a safety net into an expense.

Order 75’s hallucinated triples recur at 0.35 because a hallucination is not in the text, and the text is what each pass reads — so the passes disagree, and voting works. A misread column recurs at about 0.9 because the ambiguity is in the schema, and every sample reads the same schema. status = 3 is just as unexplained on the ninth attempt as on the first. Nothing about the method changed; the population moved across the break-even, and the method’s sign changed with it.

Two honest riders. The break-even is not a cliff you can stand next to — at a recurrence of 0.45, forty-one samples still leave a quarter of the errors uncaught, which is why order 75 needed a deliberate agreement bar rather than just more passes. And 0.9 is an input to this model, not a measurement of your database. It is the number to go and measure, because the entire verdict on voting hangs off which side of ½ it lands on.

Back-translation is the other reflex, and its catch rate is linear in independence for the reason slot 3 gives: it can only ever surface a misreading the checker did not independently make.

The 92% that a self-check confirms is worse than an unchecked query, incidentally, because a user has now been shown an English sentence describing the wrong query and has agreed with it.

On total accuracy, writing the schema down is worth about one model generation — 81.01% against 81.55%. On the class that ships it is worth 4.55× against four generations’ 1.92×, and the two levers multiply rather than overlap. The cost objection is real and small: the documented schema is a 3.9× larger prompt that costs 1.6× per question, because it is byte-identical on every question and caches.

Note the honest exception in the second row. Declaring foreign keys improves accuracy by five and a half points and raises the silent share, because it fixes more binding errors than meaning errors. Schema work only reliably attacks the dangerous class when it is documenting meaning, and a lesson claiming every schema improvement helps every metric would be selling something.

06

The trade-off: what this buys, stated as fairly as the arithmetic allows

Every check on that list costs coverage, and the marginal trade is worse than the totals suggest. Past the two cheap checks — parse and shape, which together catch 249.8 errors per 1,000 and turn away 25.2 correct answers — the rest of the gauntlet catches 29 more errors and turns away 87 more correct answers to do it, for a running total of 113. The shape check calls every legitimately empty answer a bug — how many refunds did we issue in Andorra is a real question with a real answer of zero. Back-translation makes people reject a correct query about one time in ten, because an English paraphrase of correct SQL can easily read wrong to someone who was thinking about it differently. A system that never ships a wrong number by never answering is not a system.

So, in order of what the arithmetic supports:

  1. Write the schema down. It is the only lever that moves the level rather than the mix, it is worth more than four model generations on the class that matters, and it caches. Do the meaning — comments, enumerated values, units, and vetted views for the questions people actually ask — not just the keys.
  2. Make the checker independent, not numerous. One call to a different vendor’s model beats five calls to the same one by fifty points on the class that ships, at a third of the price. If you can afford a person for the queries that matter, that is 72%.
  3. Show the query only where it can be read. 76.5% with analysts, 12.8% on a business dashboard. This is the strongest single check available and it does not scale past the people who can read SQL — which makes it the right check for a data team’s own tooling and close to decoration on an executive dashboard.
  4. Let it refuse. “Two columns could mean active and they disagree — which do you want?” is a better product than a number, and it is the only response that has the schema’s ambiguity in the right place: in front of the person who knows the answer.
  5. Contain it with the grant, not the prompt. A read-only role makes a class of outcome impossible. A sentence asking the model not to write makes it unlikely. Use a per-tenant view rather than a tenant predicate the model has to remember, a statement timeout, and a row cap. This is order 55 arriving in a new place: a generated query completes the injection trifecta by construction — private data, untrusted content the moment a result row is read back into the prompt, and the query itself as the channel — and a filter that stops 95% of attempts has been beaten at even odds by the 14th question.

And the honest limits of everything above. The per-generation factors are extrapolations from two benchmark generations, not measurements of the future; the direction is robust and the exact 5.42× is not. The 0.9 recurrence is the number the verdict on voting hangs on and it is an input — measure your own by generating five queries for fifty real questions and counting how often the wrong ones agree. The three classes are cleaner than reality, where a query can return the wrong rows and too few of them. And this lesson has said nothing about the case where text-to-query is straightforwardly good: an analyst who can read SQL, exploring a documented schema through a read-only role, using the model to skip the part where they remember the join. That is a real use, the silent class barely matters there because the reader is the check, and nothing here argues against it. What it argues against is putting the same component behind a number on a slide.

07

In the wild

The metric and what it hides. Spider established execution accuracy as the reported number for text-to-SQL, and BIRD was built specifically because scores on curated schemas were not predicting behaviour on real databases — dirty values, external knowledge, business logic in the data. The twenty-three-point gap between them, on the same systems, is the most direct published measurement of the schema’s contribution, and BIRD’s human baseline is what makes it a schema problem rather than a ceiling. Neither leaderboard reports the split of the residual into will-not-run, returns-nothing, and answers-a-different-question, which is the number an operator needs and the reason this lesson has to model it.

The industry already agreed with slot 6, point 1. Every serious product in this space has converged on not generating SQL against raw tables. dbt’s metrics layer, LookML, Cube, and the semantic layers inside the warehouse vendors’ own assistants all exist to put a curated, documented, named set of measures and dimensions between the question and the physical schema. That is the vetted views rung, built as a product category. The convergence is evidence, and the direction of travel is the opposite of “wait for a better model”.

Self-consistency, and where it was validated. The technique comes from Wang et al.’s self-consistency work on chain-of-thought reasoning, where sampled reasoning paths that reach the same answer by different routes genuinely corroborate each other. The assumption doing the work is that errors are diverse. Carrying it to generated SQL against an ambiguous schema violates that assumption in the specific way this lesson quantifies, and the failure is silent: the vote returns a confident unanimous result set, which is exactly what a monitoring dashboard reads as health.

Independent back-translation. The protocol is codified in the ISPOR task force report on translating patient-reported outcome measures and in the WHO’s process for its own instruments, and both specify a back-translator who has not seen the source. Reading a translation protocol is a faster route to the right architecture for a text-to-query checker than reading a text-to-SQL paper, because the protocol was written by people who had already paid for the correlated-checker mistake.

Grants. Read-only roles, per-tenant views, statement timeouts and row caps are ordinary database administration, older than this problem and unchanged by it. The failure mode worth naming is a team that implements all of the prompt-level controls and none of these, because the prompt-level ones are the ones that appear in the papers.

  • Spider — Yu et al., Spider: A Large-Scale Human-Labeled Dataset for Complex and Cross-Domain Semantic Parsing and Text-to-SQL Task, 2018. The origin of execution accuracy as the reported number, and of the curated-schema regime.
  • BIRD — Li et al., Can LLM Already Serve as A Database Interface? A BIg Bench for Large-Scale Database Grounded Text-to-SQLs, 2023. Real databases, dirty values, external knowledge, and the human baseline that makes the gap a schema property rather than a ceiling.
  • Self-consistency — Wang et al., Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models, 2022. The technique, and the diversity-of-errors assumption it rests on.
  • ISPOR translation and cultural adaptation task force — Wild et al., Principles of Good Practice for the Translation and Cultural Adaptation Process for Patient-Reported Outcomes (PRO) Measures, 2005; and the WHO process for translation and adaptation of instruments. Both specify an independent back-translator who has not seen the source text.
  • Semantic layers — dbt metrics / MetricFlow, LookML, and Cube as shipped products. The vetted views rung of the schema ladder, as a category.

A prompt cannot revoke a permission — a read-only role, a per-tenant view, a statement timeout and a row cap are the controls, and a filter that stops 95% of injection attempts has been beaten at even odds by the 14th question

Score yourself on the list, not on how much you wrote. A missing item is a located gap, which is worth more than a page of recall that skirted it.

  1. Cued recall

    Your text-to-SQL system reports 91% execution accuracy, up from 63% last year. Your incident count from wrong dashboard numbers has not fallen. Is one of the two numbers wrong?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    Neither. Accuracy counts errors and incidents count silent errors, and those two populations come apart under exactly this kind of improvement. At 63% accurate, 17 of every 100 questions produced a query that would not run and 12 produced a plausible wrong answer. At 91%, 0.3 will not run and 7.4 are plausible wrong answers. The total error rate fell by 4.3×; the errors that reach a human as a number fell by 1.6×. The mix went from 32% silent to 85% silent, because the class a better model fixes is the class that announced itself.
    Did you have that?
  2. Cued recall

    You are generating five queries per question and majority-voting the result sets. Someone proposes fifteen for more confidence. What does that buy?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    On the class that matters, less than nothing. A majority vote is a coin flip at a recurrence of exactly ½, at every odd sample count. Dialect slips recur at ~0.2, well below the line, and fifteen samples take the catch rate from 94.2% to 99.6%. A misread column recurs at ~0.9, above the line, and the same change takes the catch rate from 0.86% to 0.00% — every extra sample makes the majority surer of the shared misreading. You would be paying 3× to ship marginally more silent errors. Spend the same two calls on a checker from a different vendor instead: 52% instead of 0.86%, at one third the price.
    Did you have that?
  3. Cued recall

    You add a back-translation step: the model turns its own SQL back into English and the user confirms it. Measured over a month, it barely helps. Why, and what is the fix?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    Because the checker and the translator misread the schema the same way. Back-translation only ever sees a misreading the checker did not independently make, so its catch rate is linear in independence: 8% for the same model on the same prompt, and the 92% it confirms is largely the original mistake restated confidently in English — which is worse than no check, because the user has now signed off. The fix is not more effort, it is a different reader: 24% if the same model is explicitly told to hunt for a misreading, 52% with another vendor's model, 72% with a person who knows the schema. The cost is identical in the first three cases.
    Did you have that?
  4. Cued recall

    You have budget for one of: a frontier model upgrade, a five-way result-set vote, or a week of an engineer writing a comment on every column and a handful of vetted views. Which buys the most safety?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    The week, and it is not close. On the class that ships, four generations of model improvement is 1.92× and the schema work is 4.55× — 12.0% of questions down to 2.64%. The vote is worse than nothing there. And the two real levers multiply rather than overlap, so the schema work is not made redundant by the upgrade you will get anyway. The cost objection is real and small: the documented schema is a 3.9× bigger prompt for 1.6× the price, because it is byte-identical on every question and caches.
    Did you have that?
  5. Cued recall

    The generated query is about to run. What actually limits the damage if the model has misread the question entirely?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    The grant, and nothing else in the prompt. A read-only role makes an entire class of outcome impossible; a sentence in the system prompt asking the model not to write makes it unlikely. Add a per-tenant view rather than a tenant predicate the model has to remember, a statement timeout, and a row cap. This is order 55's point arriving in a new place: a generated query completes the injection trifecta by construction — private data, untrusted content the moment a row is read back into the prompt, and the query itself as the channel — and a filter that stops 95% of attempts is beaten at even odds by the 14th question.
    Did you have that?
  6. Near transfer

    Same shape, different field: professional human translation of a survey instrument or a consent form, where a mistranslation is discovered later and expensively. What did that field converge on, and what does it say about who should check a machine's output?

    How sure are you, before you look?
    It converged on independent back-translation, and the independence is the load-bearing part of the protocol rather than a detail of it. The established guidance — ISPOR's task force report on translating patient-reported outcome measures, and the WHO's process for instrument translation — specifies that the back-translator must be a different person who has not seen the source text, and typically that two forward translators work independently before a reconciliation step. The reason is exactly this lesson's arithmetic: a translator who back-translates their own work reproduces their own misreading, so the check confirms rather than tests. There is a second transfer worth having. That field also treats a fluent mistranslation as the dangerous one and an awkward or obviously-broken rendering as benign, because the awkward one gets caught. And its actual remedy for the hardest cases is not a better translator at all — it is cognitive debriefing, where the instrument is tried on real respondents and their interpretation is compared with the intended one. That is the same move as showing the SQL to someone who can read it, and it has the same limitation: it does not scale, so it is spent on the items that matter rather than on all of them.
    Did you have that?
  7. Far transfer

    The abstraction is: a system's quality is measured as one aggregate over failure modes with radically different detectability, so improving the aggregate selectively removes the detectable failures and leaves a residue that is more dangerous per unit than what you started with. Name instances outside software.

    How sure are you, before you look?
    Aviation and automation-induced error. Decades of automation removed the failure modes that announced themselves — fuel miscalculation, controlled flight into terrain, mechanical failure — and the accident rate fell by orders of magnitude. The residue is dominated by mode confusion: the aircraft did exactly what it was told and what it was told was not what the crew intended, and nothing in the cockpit contradicted them. The field's response is instructive because it matches slot 6: not "better autopilots" but explicit mode annunciation, callouts that force a second person to independently state what the aircraft is doing, and training aimed specifically at the undetectable class. Others: antibiotics and resistance, where treating the susceptible population leaves a residue that is harder to detect and worse per case; industrial safety, where guarding machinery removed the lacerations and left musculoskeletal and chemical injuries with years-long latency, which is why exposure monitoring replaced injury counting; drug trial endpoints, where a composite endpoint improves while the component that matters does not, and the remedy is pre-specified per-component reporting; spell-checkers, whose adoption eliminated misspellings and raised the share of remaining errors that are correctly-spelled wrong words — the errors a reader's eye skates over; and credit fraud controls, where blocking the crude patterns leaves account-takeover and first-party fraud, which look like legitimate customer behaviour by construction. In every instance the aggregate metric improved honestly, the mix inverted, and the fix was a separate measurement of the undetectable class plus a second independent party — never a better version of the thing that was already working.
    Did you have that?

Graded items get a due date on an expanding ladder — 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 90, 180 days — and land in review mixed with every other lesson's items. Mixed, not grouped: blocking one topic teaches you what its problems have in common, while interleaving forces you to tell them apart, which is the skill that actually fails in an interview and in production. Nothing is marked mastered today — same-day correctness measures how accessible a thing is right now, not how well you learned it.
